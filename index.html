The Knowledge to Execution Platform

Three pillars,
one problem

Companies do not have a training problem. They have a knowledge transfer problem — everything they collectively know is trapped in documents nobody reads, videos nobody finishes, and the heads of the four people who are always in meetings. Lupo.ai attacks that in three moves.

01

Create

The content factory. You ship a feature and you have a deck explaining it — before, turning that into a video took hours or days. Here it takes minutes. Record your screen with no studio, no lighting, no second take: out comes clean narration with the stutters gone, the accent evened out, and a German or Spanish version if you want one.

Weeks to minutes
02

Learn

An LMS with AI agents all over it. Push what you made out to employees or customers, then see who actually watched, quiz them, and measure adoption. And when someone does not want to sit through ten minutes to find one answer, they just ask the agent — mid-video, in their own words — and get it.

Distribute, monitor, measure
03

Knowledge

Throw everything in — documentation, processes, SOPs, the standards of behaviour nobody has read since onboarding. Someone asks a question, agents search across all of it and hand back the exact procedure to follow. Not a list of links. The steps.

The answer, not the search results
I'm not a visionary. I'm just a guy who's been in the learning space for 24 years. — Xavier Morera, on a call in July 2026

Which is exactly the point. I did not arrive at the future of adult education through a whiteboard exercise. I got here teaching in Microsoft Technology Centers from Seoul to Stockholm, running Intel's Itanium Developer Forums and HP's developer events, authoring 64 courses on Pluralsight alone that have been watched by nearly half a million people — and spending seven years building a content pipeline for one selfish reason: if I could make courses faster, I could make more of them.

Then a consulting client asked to use it. That client was acquired by Accenture. Not many companies can say their first customer became the largest consultancy on earth — and that is when the tool stopped being my moat and started being the product.

Education is how people get ahead in life. — The reason any of this matters

If you can take what someone already knows how to do and let them teach it to others, they can build themselves out of poverty. That is not a marketing line bolted onto a software company — it is the reason the software exists.

It is also why the conversations I care most about right now are with universities and institutions working on micro-credentials, where the same platform that onboards a sales team could put a recognised, portable skill in the hands of someone with no other route to one.

Signature Essay

The way of the
road warrior

It starts in New Orleans in April 2002 and it has not really stopped since. Nineteen Microsoft Technology Center engagements across fourteen cities and four continents. Intel's Itanium Developer Forums in New Jersey, Washington, San Diego and Houston. HP's Developer Forum in Toronto and their headquarters in Mexico City. Then Solr, Cloudera, Spark Summit, Strata, and a decade of Pluralsight.

I kept the list. Every trip, every city, twenty-one years of it — because the mileage is not the point, but it is the receipt. This is what it actually takes to learn how adults learn.
